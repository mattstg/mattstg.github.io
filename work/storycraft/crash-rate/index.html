Cutting the crash rate by 73%

Storycraft is a live-ops iOS game built on worlds its players make. When I took over the game client, 5.5% of sessions ended in a crash. When I left, 1.5% did.

Role
Owner of the game client; I built the tooling and the fixes below
When
Aug 2025 – Oct 2026
Platform
iOS, mostly iPhone 13 and 14 class devices
Stack
Unity, C#, Sentry, BigQuery, MetricKit, a CDN
Result
5.5% → 1.5% of sessions crashing (crash-free sessions 94.5% → 98.5%)

Why it crashed

Every world is built from images its players uploaded, often hundreds of unique ones per world. None of them can ship in the build, be packed into atlases or be compressed ahead of time, because they don't exist until a player makes them. On a typical iPhone that adds up fast.

Two failures came out of that:

  • Out-of-memory kills. iOS ends an app that uses too much memory, with no crash report from inside the app.
  • Watchdog terminations. World loading ran on the main thread. A big world kept the app from responding long enough that iOS ended it.

Neither shows up well in a normal crash reporter, which was part of the problem.

Measure first

Before changing anything I made the crashes countable, starting with written rules for what counts as a crash, a freeze and a memory kill.

  1. Crash census. A record kept for every launch that notes whether the previous session ended cleanly. That catches out-of-memory kills, which leave no report, plus a watchdog for stalls and Apple's MetricKit data.
  2. Memory tooling. What memory is made of and how it changes over a session, detection of iOS memory-pressure kills, and a low-RAM mode to reproduce them on a developer's phone.
  3. Error capture and pipelines. A Sentry wrapper with fingerprinting and boot-phase timing, Sentry data piped into BigQuery, and views by crash type, device tier and release.
  4. Crash reports from players. After a crash, the next launch uploads zipped traces and logs.

The fixes

  • Memory management systems, including a three-tier image cache with a ledger of what each image costs in memory.
  • Texture settings fixed so images don't keep a second, CPU-side copy in memory.
  • Image processing moved to the server, with processed images cached and served through a CDN, so the phone receives images ready to use.
  • Async, multithreaded world loading, off the main thread, so iOS no longer sees the app as frozen.
  • A thermal and framerate governor that lowers the framerate when the phone heats up.

Result: crash rate down from 5.5% to 1.5% of sessions. Most out-of-memory kills and watchdog terminations are gone, on the same devices and with the same player-made worlds.
